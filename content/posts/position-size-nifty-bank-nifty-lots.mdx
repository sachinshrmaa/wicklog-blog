> **TL;DR:** Under the 1% rule, number of lots = (capital × 1%) ÷ (stop distance × lot size). With Nifty at 65 units per lot and Bank Nifty at 30, a ₹5 lakh account risking ₹5,000 can trade 3 Nifty option lots with a ₹20 premium stop. If one lot's risk exceeds 1%, the right size is zero, not one.

Most blown-up F&O accounts didn't die from a bad strategy. They died from one good-looking trade sized at five lots when the account could carry one.

Lot sizing in India has a quirk that global position-sizing guides miss: you can't buy 2.4 lots. Exchange lot sizes force your risk into fixed steps, and on a small account, the smallest step is often bigger than 1%. This guide works through the math with real Nifty and Bank Nifty numbers.

## The Root Problem: Traders Size by Conviction, Not by Stop Distance

**Most retail traders pick their lot count first and their stop-loss second, which makes risk per trade random.** The same "2 lots" can risk ₹1,300 on one trade and ₹9,000 on the next, depending on where the stop happens to be.

The fix is to reverse the order: decide the rupee risk, find the stop the chart gives you, and let the math produce the lot count.

Why 1%? Because losing streaks are normal, and recovery is asymmetric.

| Risk per trade | Account after 10 straight losses | Gain needed to recover |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| 1% | −9.6% | +10.6% |
| 2% | −18.3% | +22.4% |
| 5% | −40.1% | +67.0% |

Ten losses in a row sounds rare. For a strategy with a 40% win rate, any given run of ten trades has about a 1-in-165 chance of being all losses. At 6–7 trades a day, that's roughly a year of trading, so expect to see it. At 5% risk, that streak leaves you needing a 67% return just to get back to where you started.

## The Breakdown: Position Size Formulas for Nifty and Bank Nifty

**Number of lots = rupee risk ÷ (stop distance per unit × lot size), always rounded down.** Everything else is plugging in numbers.

### The inputs

- **Capital:** the money in your trading account, not your net worth.
- **Risk %:** 0.5–1% for most active traders; 2% is an upper limit.
- **Stop distance:** entry price minus stop price, in premium points for options or index points for futures.
- **Lot size:** NSE currently sets **Nifty at 65** and **Bank Nifty at 30** units per lot, effective from the January 2026 series. [NEEDS SOURCE: confirm against NSE's latest lot size circular before publishing; NSE revises these periodically]

### Example 1: Nifty option buyer, ₹5 lakh account

- Capital: ₹5,00,000 at 1% risk = **₹5,000**
- Buy a Nifty call at ₹140, stop at ₹120 → stop distance **₹20**
- Risk per lot: ₹20 × 65 = **₹1,300**
- Lots: ₹5,000 ÷ ₹1,300 = 3.85 → **3 lots** (₹3,900 at risk)

### Example 2: Bank Nifty option buyer, same account

- Risk budget: **₹5,000**
- Buy a Bank Nifty put at ₹310, stop at ₹250 → stop distance **₹60**
- Risk per lot: ₹60 × 30 = **₹1,800**
- Lots: ₹5,000 ÷ ₹1,800 = 2.78 → **2 lots** (₹3,600 at risk)

### Example 3: Nifty futures, same account

- Risk budget: **₹5,000**
- Long at 25,000, stop at 24,940 → stop distance **60 points**
- Risk per lot: 60 × 65 = **₹3,900**
- Lots: ₹5,000 ÷ ₹3,900 = 1.28 → **1 lot**

Futures risk adds up fast. A 100-point stop on one Nifty futures lot is ₹6,500, already over 1% of a ₹5 lakh account.

### Add charges to your risk

**Your real loss on a stopped-out trade is the stop distance plus charges, so include roughly ₹80–100 per option round trip in your risk budget.** For a Nifty option trade of 2 lots, Zerodha-style charges (₹40 brokerage, 0.15% STT on the sell, exchange fees, stamp duty and GST) come to about ₹85. On futures, the April 2026 STT increase to 0.05% pushed a one-lot Nifty round trip to roughly ₹950.

On small risk budgets this matters. If you risk ₹1,000 per trade, ₹90 of charges is 9% of it.

## The Lot-Size Trap: When One Lot Is Already Too Big

**If the risk on a single lot exceeds your 1% budget, the correct position size is zero.** Taking one lot "because you can't trade less" is how small accounts end up risking 3–5% per trade without noticing.

Maximum stop distance you can afford for one lot at 1% risk:

| Account size | 1% risk | Max stop, 1 Nifty lot (65) | Max stop, 1 Bank Nifty lot (30) |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| ₹1,00,000 | ₹1,000 | ₹15.4 per unit | ₹33.3 per unit |
| ₹3,00,000 | ₹3,000 | ₹46.2 per unit | ₹100.0 per unit |
| ₹5,00,000 | ₹5,000 | ₹76.9 per unit | ₹166.7 per unit |
| ₹10,00,000 | ₹10,000 | ₹153.8 per unit | ₹333.3 per unit |

On a ₹1 lakh account, a Nifty option trade whose chart stop is ₹25 away risks ₹1,625, which is 1.6%. Your choices are:

1. **Skip the trade.** Not every setup fits your account.
2. **Choose a different strike** where a ₹15 stop is structurally valid. Moving the stop closer just to fit the math only guarantees more stop-outs.
3. **Accept a higher risk %** knowingly, and cap the number of such trades.

What you should not do is trade one lot and pretend it's 1%.

### Stops are not guarantees

Option premiums can gap through your stop, especially around big moves or close to expiry. Size assuming some slippage, and treat the formula's output as a ceiling.

## Key Takeaways

- **Lots = rupee risk ÷ (stop distance × lot size), rounded down.** Pick risk first, lots last.
- **At Nifty 65 and Bank Nifty 30 per lot,** a ₹1 lakh account can only afford a ₹15 Nifty option stop on one lot at 1% risk.
- **If one lot breaks your 1% budget, the answer is zero lots,** a different strike, or a deliberate exception, never a quiet 3% bet.
- **Budget charges into risk.** About ₹85 per option round trip and roughly ₹950 per Nifty futures round trip after the April 2026 STT hike.
- **Verify with data.** Average loss larger than planned risk means your stops or your sizing are slipping.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many lots of Nifty should I trade with ₹1 lakh capital?

At 1% risk, ₹1 lakh gives a ₹1,000 risk budget, so one Nifty lot of 65 only fits if your stop is within about ₹15 per unit. For most option setups that means trading one lot at most, and skipping trades with wider stops. For Nifty futures, one lot needs a stop under about 15 points, which is rarely realistic.

### What is the 1% rule in trading?

The 1% rule means never risking more than 1% of your trading capital on a single trade, measured from entry to stop-loss. It keeps a normal losing streak, such as 10 losses in a row, to a drawdown of under 10%.
