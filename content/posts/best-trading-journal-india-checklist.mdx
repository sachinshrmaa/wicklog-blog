## The Root Problem: Most Journals Fail on Data, Not Features

**Indian traders abandon journals because the data going in is wrong or takes too long to enter, not because the dashboard lacks charts.** A journal with 200 metrics built on miscounted trades and gross P&L is 200 wrong numbers.

Three Indian-specific facts make this harder than it looks:

- **Broker exports list fills, not trades.** A 4-lot Nifty option order can appear as three partial fills; a scale-out appears as two sells.
- **F&O charges are large and multi-part.** Brokerage, STT, exchange charges, SEBI fees, stamp duty and 18% GST, with STT on option premium sales raised to 0.15% and on futures sales to 0.05% from 1 April 2026.
- **Symbols carry structure.** An option symbol encodes underlying, expiry, strike and CE/PE. Without parsing, strike-level or expiry-day analysis is impossible.

So start with data, then look at analysis.

## The Breakdown: 9 Checks for Any Trading Journal in India

### Check 1: Does it import from the broker you actually use?

**A journal is only useful if your trades arrive in it without retyping.** Look for two things: auto-sync (connect once, sync on demand) and tradebook file import (upload what your broker exports).

Test it with last month's real tradebook, not the demo data. WickLog auto-syncs Zerodha Kite and Dhan through their official OAuth logins, with Groww and Angel One in beta, and imports Zerodha and Dhan tradebook files on the free plan.

### Check 2: Does it turn fills into trades correctly?

**Import a day where you scaled in or out, then count the trades.** If the journal shows more trades than you took, its win rate and average win are wrong.

Good matching merges partial fills of one order, matches buys and sells first-in-first-out per symbol, and keeps intraday (MIS) separate from delivery (CNC) and margin (MTF) positions. It should also handle shorts and positions that flip direction.

### Check 3: Does it calculate Indian charges per trade?

**Your journal's net P&L should be within a few rupees of your contract note.** Compare one busy day.

A single 2-lot Nifty option round trip on Zerodha costs about ₹86 in charges at current rates. At 8 trades a day, that's roughly ₹13,800 a month, enough to turn a gross-flat month into a real loss. A journal that only stores gross P&L, or makes you type one "fees" number, hides exactly this.

### Check 4: Does it understand F&O instruments?

**It should recognise options and futures, CE/PE, lot quantities and product types without manual columns.** Trading Nifty weekly options on expiry Tuesdays? You want to filter by that, not reconstruct it in a pivot table.

### Check 5: Does it handle partial exits and month-end correctly?

**Book half a position this month and half next month; the P&L should land in the months it was realised.** Many spreadsheets and simple journals assign the whole trade to the entry date, which distorts monthly results.

### Check 6: Is F&O on the free plan, and is pricing in rupees?

**Several global journals restrict free plans to stocks only or a handful of trades, and price paid tiers in dollars.** Check exactly what the free tier includes for options and futures.

### Check 7: Is broker access read-only, and is your data encrypted?

**A journal never needs permission to place orders or move funds.** Confirm that access is read-only and ask how stored data is protected.

WickLog only reads trades, holdings, positions and margins. Trade details, journal content and broker credentials are encrypted at rest with AES-GCM. That protects against casual reads of the database; it isn't end-to-end encryption, and we don't claim it is.

### Check 8: Does it answer the review questions you'll actually ask?

**The useful questions are about when, what and how you lose, not just how much.** Look for:

- Win rate and P&L by entry hour (the 12:00–1:30 PM chop question)
- A daily P&L calendar to spot red-day clusters
- Performance by setup or strategy tag
- Profit factor, expectancy and max drawdown, not just win rate
- Optionally, an AI analyst that answers questions from your own trades

### Check 9: Can you get your data out?

**You should be able to export every trade, in a format your CA can use.** Avoid any tool that locks your history in. WickLog exports all trades to a formatted `.xlsx`.

## Key Takeaways

- **Test with your real tradebook,** not demo data. Data quality decides everything else.
- **Count trades after import.** More trades than you took means fill matching is broken.
- **Match net P&L to your contract note.** If it doesn't, the journal is reporting gross numbers.
- **Check what the free plan covers for F&O,** and what the paid plan costs in rupees.
- **Pick by your broker first.** A great journal without your broker is a data-entry job.
