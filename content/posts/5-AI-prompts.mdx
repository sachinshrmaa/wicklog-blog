## Step-by-Step Walkthrough

### Step 1: Find your revenge-trading pattern
Ask Ticker AI: *"Show me all trades I entered within 15 minutes of a stop-loss hit, and compare their average risk-reward to my overall average."*

This isolates the specific behavior of jumping back into the market immediately after a loss, before you've had time to reset. The reasoning: revenge trades are rarely planned, so they almost always carry weaker risk-reward setups than your baseline — this prompt gives you the number instead of the feeling. If the average R:R on these trades is meaningfully lower than your account average, you have a quantified leak, not a suspicion.

### Step 2: Isolate your worst time-of-day window
Ask Ticker AI: *"Break down my win rate and average P&L by hour of day, from 9:15 AM to 3:30 PM."*

Most retail traders have one dead zone — often the 12 PM to 1:30 PM lull, or the volatile last 15 minutes before close — where their edge disappears entirely. Hourly segmentation turns a vague feeling ("I always lose in the afternoon") into an exact window you can simply stop trading in.

### Step 3: Audit your position sizing discipline
Ask Ticker AI: *"List trades where my position size was larger than my prior 10-trade average, and show whether they came after 2 or more consecutive wins."*

This tests for the most common and most expensive leak: sizing up out of overconfidence rather than a rules-based reason. Cross-check what Ticker AI returns against WickLog's Position Size calculator to see what your risk exposure *should* have been on those specific trades versus what it actually was.

### Step 4: Find your weakest instrument or setup type
Ask Ticker AI: *"Compare my Expectancy across each instrument and strategy tag I've logged."*

Expectancy (average win × win rate − average loss × loss rate) tells you which setups are actually profitable over time versus which just feel good because of a few memorable wins. A trader who's net profitable overall can still be bleeding money on one specific instrument — like BankNifty weekly options — while their Nifty swing trades carry the whole account.

### Step 5: Check if your stated rules match your actual behavior
Ask Ticker AI: *"Search my trade notes for any mention of moving my stop-loss, and show the P&L impact of those trades versus trades where the stop stayed fixed."*

This only works if you've been logging a brief note per trade — it's the one prompt that needs your own written record, not just the numeric data. It closes the loop between what you *say* your rule is and what your logged behavior actually shows.

## Common Mistakes & Troubleshooting

- **"Ticker AI says I don't have enough data"** — This usually means fewer than 15-20 trades are imported for the filter you asked about (e.g., a specific instrument). Broaden the date range or the instrument filter before concluding there's no pattern.
- **CSV import shows duplicate or missing trades** — Usually caused by re-uploading an overlapping date range from Kite or Groww, or a contract note with merged multi-leg F&O orders. Re-export a clean, non-overlapping date range from your broker and re-import.
- **Prompt returns a pattern with only 3-4 trades** — Treat this as a hypothesis, not a conclusion. A leak needs a large enough sample to rule out coincidence; keep logging and re-run the same prompt in a few weeks.
- **Notes field is empty, so behavior-based prompts (Step 5) return nothing** — Ticker AI can only search what you've written. Start adding a one-line note per trade going forward if you want this category of leak detection to work.
