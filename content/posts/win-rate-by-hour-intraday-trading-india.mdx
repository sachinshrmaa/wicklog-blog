> **TL;DR:** Many Indian intraday traders make money in the first hour after 9:15 AM IST and give it back between noon and 1:30 PM. Splitting your win rate and net P&L by entry hour shows whether that's you. If one window loses consistently over 50+ trades, trading less in it is usually the cheapest edge you'll ever find.

It's 9:52 AM. You caught the opening move in Nifty, booked ₹8,000, and the day is made. By 1:15 PM you've taken six more trades in a market that hasn't gone anywhere for two hours, and you're up ₹1,200.

Nothing went dramatically wrong. You just kept trading after your edge stopped showing up. Most traders never notice because their P&L is reported per day, not per hour.

## The Root Problem: Your Edge Has Business Hours, Your Trading Doesn't

**Intraday setups depend on volatility and participation, both of which change through the trading day, but most traders apply the same rules from 9:15 AM to 3:30 PM.** A breakout strategy that works when volume is heavy fails repeatedly in a quiet, range-bound market.

A typical NSE cash and F&O session (IST) has distinct phases:

| Window | What usually happens |
| :--- | :--- |
| 9:15–9:30 AM | Opening price discovery, gaps getting filled or extended, wide spreads in options |
| 9:30–11:00 AM | Trend days often reveal themselves; strongest momentum for many setups |
| 11:00 AM–12:00 PM | Moves slow; first pullbacks and failed breakouts |
| 12:00–1:30 PM | Lower participation, tighter ranges, frequent false breaks ("lunch chop") |
| 1:30–3:00 PM | Activity returns as traders position for the close |
| 3:00–3:30 PM | Square-off flows, intraday positions closed, expiry-day premium crush |

These are tendencies, not laws. Your numbers are the only ones that matter, and they can differ completely from this table. That's why you measure.

### Why the midday window costs more than it looks

Three effects stack up in a slow market:

- **Lower win rate.** Breakout and momentum setups fail more often when there's no follow-through.
- **More trades.** Boredom and the urge to "make back" a small loss push trade count up.
- **Charges on every trade.** Each Nifty option round trip of 2 lots costs roughly ₹85 in brokerage, STT (0.15% on the premium sold since April 2026), exchange fees, stamp duty and GST. Ten extra midday trades a week is over ₹3,000 a month in charges alone.

## The Breakdown: Reading Your Win Rate by Hour

**Group closed trades by entry hour, then compare trade count, win rate and net P&L after charges for each window.** Net P&L is the column that decides; win rate explains why.

### An illustrative month

*The following is a hypothetical Nifty options trader, not a real account.* 180 trades in a month, net of charges:

| Entry window | Trades | Win rate | Net P&L |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 9:15–10:00 AM | 52 | 56% | +₹21,400 |
| 10:00–11:00 AM | 38 | 50% | +₹6,200 |
| 11:00 AM–12:00 PM | 27 | 41% | −₹4,800 |
| 12:00–1:30 PM | 41 | 34% | −₹14,900 |
| 1:30–3:30 PM | 22 | 45% | −₹1,300 |
| **Total** | **180** | **46%** | **+₹6,600** |

Read it the way you'd read a P&L statement for a business:

- **The first 105 minutes made ₹27,600.** That is the actual edge.
- **Everything after 11 AM lost ₹21,000.** The trader worked six hours to keep a quarter of the morning's profit.
- **The 12:00–1:30 PM window alone took 23% of all trades** and lost more than the 10–11 AM window made.

Remove the midday window and the month goes from +₹6,600 to **+₹21,500**, with 41 fewer trades and roughly ₹3,500 less in charges. Stop trading after 11 AM entirely and it's +₹27,600.

### Don't over-read small samples

**Treat an hourly bucket as meaningful only after roughly 30–50 trades in it.** With 8 trades in the 3 PM window, one lucky expiry trade can flip the result. Look at 2–3 months together before you change your rules, and compare like with like: expiry Tuesdays (Nifty's weekly expiry) behave differently from other days.

### Check win rate and payoff together

A 34% win rate isn't automatically bad. A trend-following setup with 1:3 reward to risk is profitable at 34%. The midday problem in the example is a low win rate *and* small winners: range-bound markets cut targets short while stops still get hit in full. Compare average win and average loss within each window, not just the win rate.

## Key Takeaways

- **Measure P&L by entry hour, not by day.** Daily P&L hides a profitable morning and an unprofitable afternoon inside the same number.
- **The midday window costs three ways:** lower win rate, more trades, and charges on every one of them.
- **Decide on net P&L after charges,** using at least 30–50 trades per window and 2–3 months of data.
- **Cutting a losing window is often the cheapest edge available:** no new strategy, just fewer bad trades.
- **Write it down and re-check it.** A playbook rule plus the same widget next month tells you whether it worked.
