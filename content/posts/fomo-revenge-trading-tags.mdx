**TL;DR:** FOMO and revenge tags are two labels you attach to trades that broke your plan: FOMO for chasing a move, revenge for trading to recover a loss. Once tagged, each label has a rupee cost you can total. In the worked example below, those two tags account for ₹65,810 of losses in a quarter.

You close a Bank Nifty trade at a ₹2,400 loss at 10:05 AM. By 10:12 you are short again at double the lots, and at 10:40 you are long because "it's going without me now." Neither trade was in your plan. Neither shows up in your broker's P&L as anything but a normal trade. That is why the same two mistakes keep coming back: nothing labels them.

## The Root Problem

Most traders keep paying for FOMO and revenge trades because the broker statement treats them like any other trade, so the cost never gets attributed to the behaviour that caused it. Without a label, a ₹1,400 revenge loss and a ₹1,400 planned stop-loss look identical.

Three things keep the cost hidden:

1. **P&L is organised by symbol and date, not by reason.** You can see what you lost on Bank Nifty, but not what you lost on trades you shouldn't have taken.
2. **The two behaviours feel justified in the moment.** FOMO feels like "momentum." Revenge feels like "getting back to even." Neither feels like a mistake until the loss is booked.
3. **Memory sorts the story by pain, not by frequency.** You remember the one big revenge loss and forget the 30 small FOMO entries that cost more in total.

Here is how the two behaviours differ:

| | FOMO trade | Revenge trade |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Price is moving and you are not in it | You just took a loss |
| Typical timing | Mid-move, after a strong candle | Within minutes of a stop-loss hit |
| Typical entry quality | Late, at a poor price | Immediate, with no setup |
| Typical size | Normal or slightly larger | Larger than the trade that lost |
| Typical result | Many small losses | Fewer, larger losses |

## The Breakdown

Tracking works when you define each tag in advance, apply it after the session, and total the rupee cost by tag. The sections below cover the definitions, the routine, the worked ₹50,000 example and the rules that follow from it.

### Define the Tags Before You Use Them

A tag is only useful if its definition is objective enough that you would label the same trade the same way twice. Write the criteria down before the session, not after seeing the loss.

| Tag | Objective test (all must be true) |
|---|---|
| **FOMO** | Entry was not a setup in your written plan, **and** price had already moved sharply before you entered, **and** the reason you gave yourself was about not missing the move |
| **Revenge** | Entry was within 15 minutes of a losing trade, **and** size was equal to or larger than that losing trade, **and** it was not a setup in your plan |
| **Planned** | The setup, entry and stop-loss were all in your plan before the trade |

The 15-minute window is a starting point, so adjust it to your style. Keep a **Planned** tag as the control group. Without it you can't tell whether your tagged trades are worse than your normal ones.

Be strict. If a trade matches a setup in your plan, tag it Planned even if you were nervous. The point is to isolate trades that broke the plan, not to punish yourself.

### The Weekly Tagging Routine

The most reliable routine is to tag trades after the 3:30 PM close from the actual trade list, not during the session and not from memory. Tagging in the middle of a drawdown is exactly when your judgement is worst.

1. **Export the day's trades.** Download the trade CSV from Zerodha Kite, Groww or Dhan.
2. **Import it into WickLog.** One-click CSV import gives you the day's trade list and P&L, so you are not reconstructing it from memory.
3. **Walk the trades in order.** For each trade, apply Planned, FOMO or Revenge using the tests above.
4. **Record the tag in your own log.** A spreadsheet or notebook works: date, time, symbol, lots, P&L, tag.
5. **Total by tag every weekend.** Count trades, total ₹ and average ₹ per tag.

### The Worked Example: Where ₹50,000 Comes From

When you total losses by tag, the cost of behaviour usually dwarfs the cost of normal losing trades. This is a hypothetical ₹5,00,000 account over one quarter (about 60 trading days), before charges.

| Tag | Trades | Avg P&L per trade | Total P&L |
|---|---|---|---|
| Planned | 260 | +₹158 | +₹41,000 |
| FOMO | 58 | −₹520 | −₹30,160 |
| Revenge | 31 | −₹1,150 | −₹35,650 |
| **All trades** | **349** | | **−₹24,810** |

The planned trades made money. The account lost ₹24,810 because 89 tagged trades, about a quarter of all trades, cost ₹65,810.

Now suppose you write one rule per tag and cut each behaviour by about 75%:

| | Before rules | After rules (75% fewer tagged trades) |
|---|---|---|
| Planned P&L | +₹41,000 | +₹41,000 |
| FOMO + Revenge P&L | −₹65,810 | −₹16,453 |
| **Quarter net (before charges)** | **−₹24,810** | **+₹24,547** |
| **Improvement** | | **≈ ₹49,357, call it ₹50,000** |

Treat these numbers as an illustration of the mechanism, not a forecast. The pattern is the useful part: the planned trades never changed, and nearly all the improvement came from taking fewer trades you never planned. ### Turn Each Tag into One Rule

Each tag should produce exactly one checkable rule, because a rule you can't verify is a wish. Write the rule against the biggest rupee number first.

| Tag | Rule | How you verify it |
|---|---|---|
| Revenge | After any stop-loss, no new entry for 30 minutes, and lot size never increases the same day | Ask Ticker AI for trades within 30 minutes of a loss. Count should fall toward zero. |
| FOMO | No entry unless the setup is in the written plan, and if price has moved more than your set threshold without you, you skip it | Count FOMO tags per week. Compare with the previous week. |
| Both | Daily trade cap and a daily max loss in ₹, set before 9:15 AM | Check how many days you broke either limit |

Pair the rules with WickLog's Position Size calculator, so lot count is decided by risk per trade and not by how you feel after a loss.

Review after four weeks. If a tag's total cost has not fallen, either the rule is too vague or you are not following it. Either way, the tag total tells you which.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between FOMO trading and revenge trading?

FOMO trading is entering because price is moving and you don't want to miss it, while revenge trading is entering because you just lost and want to recover. FOMO is triggered by someone else's profit, usually a move you missed. Revenge is triggered by your own loss, usually within minutes of a stop-loss hit, and often with larger size.

### How do I track FOMO and revenge trades?

Define each tag with an objective test, then label every trade after the session using your actual trade list. Record date, time, symbol, lots, P&L and tag in a spreadsheet or notebook, and total the ₹ cost by tag each week. Keep a Planned tag as a comparison group so you can see how much worse the tagged trades are.
