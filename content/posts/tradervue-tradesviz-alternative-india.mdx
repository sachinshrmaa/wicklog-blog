## The Root Problem: A Journal That Gets Your Net P&L Wrong Is Worse Than No Journal

**An Indian F&O trader's journal is only useful if it shows P&L after charges, and global journals were not designed around Indian charges.** Every option round trip on NSE carries six separate costs: brokerage, Securities Transaction Tax (STT), exchange transaction charges, SEBI turnover fees, stamp duty, and 18% GST on brokerage and exchange fees.

Those costs got heavier this year. From **1 April 2026**, Budget 2026 raised STT on option premium sales from 0.10% to 0.15% and on futures sales from 0.02% to 0.05%. For high-frequency F&O traders, that moved the breakeven line up again.

Here is what that looks like on a single, ordinary Nifty options trade.

### Worked example: one Nifty option scalp on Zerodha

You buy 2 lots of a Nifty weekly call (lot size 65, so 130 quantity) at ₹120 and sell at ₹128.

| Line item | Calculation | Amount |
| :--- | :--- | ---: |
| Gross P&L | (128 − 120) × 130 | **+₹1,040.00** |
| Brokerage | ₹20 × 2 orders | −₹40.00 |
| STT | 0.15% × ₹16,640 sell premium | −₹24.96 |
| Exchange transaction charges | 0.03503% (NSE) × ₹32,240 premium turnover | −₹11.29 |
| SEBI fees | ₹10 per crore of turnover | −₹0.03 |
| Stamp duty | 0.003% × ₹15,600 buy premium | −₹0.47 |
| GST | 18% × (brokerage + exchange + SEBI) | −₹9.24 |
| **Net P&L** | | **≈ +₹954** |

About ₹86 of charges on one trade looks harmless. Now scale it.

Say you take 8 trades a day, win half of them, and your average winner and loser are the same ₹1,040 size. Your gross P&L is roughly flat. Your charges are roughly ₹690 a day. Over 20 trading sessions, that is **about ₹13,800 lost in a month in which your spreadsheet says you broke even.**

A journal that stores only gross P&L, or asks you to type a single "fees" number per trade, hides exactly this leak. That is the core test for any trading journal in India: does it compute net P&L the way your contract note does, without you doing the math?

### Tradervue: solid for US traders, a manual-entry job for Indian ones

**Tradervue does not list native support for any Indian broker, so Indian traders import via generic CSV mapping and enter charges themselves.** Its reporting is mature, and if you also trade US stocks through a US broker, it is a reasonable pick for that account.

For NSE F&O, the problems stack up:

- **No NSE symbol logic.** Option symbols like `NIFTY25OCT25000CE` need to be parsed into underlying, expiry, strike and CE/PE before strike-level or expiry-day analysis means anything.
- **Fees are a number you supply.** You calculate STT, exchange charges and GST per trade from your contract note, or you skip it and look at gross P&L.
- **The free plan caps you at 30 trades a month.** An active Bank Nifty scalper can cross that in two sessions.
- **Dollar pricing.** Silver at $29.95/month works out to well over ₹2,500 a month, before forex markup on your card, which is several times the cost of an Indian-built journal.

### TradesViz: the strongest global option for India, with paywalls where F&O traders need it

**TradesViz is the most India-capable global journal: it auto-syncs Zerodha, Dhan and Angel One and tracks Indian charges.** If you trade Indian and US markets from one journal, or want hundreds of chart types, it deserves a serious look.

The catches for an Indian F&O trader are specific:

- **The free plan is stocks-only.** Options and futures need Pro ($19.99/month) or Platinum.
- **Auto-sync and AI tools sit on paid plans.** The free tier excludes both.
- **Zerodha history.** [TradesViz's own help article](https://www.tradesviz.com/blog/auto-import-zerodha/) says its Zerodha API sync only returns trades from the current day, so you need to sync every trading day or fall back to file import.
- **Breadth over focus.** Supporting 250+ brokers worldwide is a strength, but it also means Indian-specific behaviour competes for attention with every other market.

**WickLog, an AI trading journal built only for Indian retail traders, treats Indian charges, symbols and brokers as the default rather than an add-on.** It doesn't support US brokers or forex, and that is the trade-off.
