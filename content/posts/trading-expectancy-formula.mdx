> **TL;DR:** Trading expectancy tells you the average ₹ you can expect to make (or lose) per trade over the long run. Formula: `(Win% × Avg Win) − (Loss% × Avg Loss)`. A positive number means your system has an edge — a high win rate with negative expectancy is still a losing strategy.

You can win 7 out of 10 trades this month and still be bleeding money. Most retail traders in NSE F&O chase win rate because it feels good — green trades feel like validation. But win rate alone tells you nothing about whether your strategy survives the next 100 trades. Expectancy does.

## The Root Problem

Retail traders obsess over accuracy because it's the number that shows up loudest in their head after every trade. A 70% win rate *sounds* like a great system. But if your average loss is 3x your average win, that system is mathematically designed to drain your account.

Here's the math nobody shows you in trading Telegram groups: expectancy is the single number that combines how often you win with how much you win or lose when you do. It's the difference between "I feel like I'm doing well" and "I have actual proof my edge exists."

Most Indian retail traders never calculate this because it requires two things they don't have: a large enough trade sample, and every trade logged with entry, exit, and P&L in one place — not scattered across a Kite contract note, a Groww statement, and a mental tally of "gut feel" wins.

## The Breakdown

### The Expectancy Formula

The formula is short, but every input matters:

```
Expectancy = (Win% × Average Win) − (Loss% × Average Loss)
```

Where:
- **Win%** = number of winning trades ÷ total trades
- **Average Win** = total profit from winning trades ÷ number of winning trades
- **Loss%** = number of losing trades ÷ total trades
- **Average Loss** = total loss from losing trades ÷ number of losing trades (as a positive number)

**Worked example — a BankNifty options scalper over 50 trades:**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Win rate | 40% (20 wins) |
| Loss rate | 60% (30 losses) |
| Average win | ₹2,400 |
| Average loss | ₹900 |

```
Expectancy = (0.40 × 2,400) − (0.60 × 900)
Expectancy = 960 − 540
Expectancy = ₹420 per trade
```

This trader wins less than half the time but has positive expectancy of ₹420/trade. Over 100 trades, that's a theoretical ₹42,000 edge — before brokerage, STT, and slippage eat into it. ### Why High Win Rate Can Still Mean Negative Expectancy

This is the trap most swing traders in Nifty and BankNifty fall into: cutting winners early ("lock in the profit") and holding losers ("it'll come back"). This inverts your risk-reward ratio and can make a 70% win-rate system a net loser.

| Scenario | Win% | Avg Win | Avg Loss | Expectancy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| High win rate, poor R:R | 70% | ₹500 | ₹1,800 | (0.70×500) − (0.30×1,800) = **−₹190** |
| Lower win rate, strong R:R | 35% | ₹2,200 | ₹600 | (0.35×2,200) − (0.65×600) = **+₹380** |

The 70%-win-rate trader is losing money every trade, on average. The 35%-win-rate trader is compounding an edge. If you only track win rate, you'd back the wrong strategy.

### Expectancy vs. Risk-Reward Ratio — What's the Difference

Risk-reward ratio tells you the *shape* of a single trade's payoff (e.g., risking ₹500 to make ₹1,500 is a 1:3 R:R). Expectancy tells you what that shape actually produces once your real win rate is applied across dozens of trades.

A strong R:R with a low enough win rate can still be net negative — expectancy is the only number that combines both variables into a single, actionable verdict on whether a strategy has a real edge.

## Key Takeaways

- Win rate alone is not a strategy verdict — expectancy is: `(Win% × Avg Win) − (Loss% × Avg Loss)`.
- A high win rate with a poor risk-reward ratio can still be net-negative expectancy.
- You need 30+ logged trades minimum for the number to mean anything statistically.
- Recalculate expectancy per strategy/setup, not just account-wide — a single strategy tag can hide a losing sub-strategy dragging down a winning one.
- Net expectancy should be calculated after brokerage and STT, not on gross P&L, since F&O charges compound fast on high-frequency setups.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a good trading expectancy value?
Any positive expectancy means your strategy has a mathematical edge over enough trades — there's no universal "good" number since it depends on your capital and trade frequency. A scalper doing 20 trades/day with ₹50 expectancy can out-earn a swing trader with ₹500 expectancy doing 2 trades/week.

### Can win rate be high but expectancy still negative?
Yes — this happens when average losses are significantly larger than average wins, even with a 70%+ win rate. It's one of the most common blind spots for traders who cut winners early and let losers run.

### How many trades do I need before expectancy numbers are reliable?
