> **TL;DR:** Revenge trading is taking unplanned trades to win back a recent loss. It shows up in data as more trades, bigger size and quick re-entries right after a loss, and as rare red days far larger than your normal ones. Measure those signals in your tradebook, then set rules that trigger before the urge does.

You get stopped out of a Nifty call at 10:12 AM. ₹2,400 gone. It was a clean setup, a fair stop. Fine.

At 10:19 you buy the put, because the market "owes you" the move. Stopped out. At 10:31 you double the size. By 11:15, the ₹2,400 loss is ₹14,000, and the morning you'll remember for the next three weeks is set.

Every trader knows this story. The problem is that it doesn't feel like revenge while it's happening. It feels like "getting back to even". That's why you need to find it in data, not memory.

## The Root Problem: Losses Hurt More Than Gains Feel Good

**Revenge trading comes from loss aversion: a loss feels roughly twice as painful as an equal gain feels good, so traders take extra risk to erase it quickly.** This is loss aversion, first described by Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky in prospect theory; their 1992 follow-up estimated that losses weigh about 2.25 times as much as equal gains.

In F&O, the setup for revenge is unusually good:

- **Leverage is instant.** Doubling size in Nifty options is one tap.
- **The market is open for 6¼ hours.** From 9:15 AM to 3:30 PM IST, there's always another candle to "make it back" on.
- **Expiry days amplify it.** Cheap, fast-moving premiums on Nifty's weekly expiry make a recovery trade look easy.
- **Every attempt costs money even when it breaks even.** Each 2-lot Nifty option round trip is about ₹86 in charges, including 0.15% STT on the premium sold.

The damage isn't from one bad trade. It's the cluster: a normal loss followed by four to six unplanned trades.

## The Breakdown: 5 Signals of Revenge Trading in Your Data

**You don't need emotion tags to detect revenge trading; timestamps, sizes and daily P&L reveal it.** Look at 2–3 months of trades for these patterns.

### Signal 1: Trade frequency spikes after a loss

Compare the number of trades in the 60 minutes after a losing trade with the 60 minutes after a winning one. If you trade noticeably more after losses, you're reacting, not following a plan.

### Signal 2: Position size grows after a loss

Check quantity on the trade immediately after a loss. If your normal size is 2 lots and post-loss trades are often 3–4, you're trying to recover faster, which is the definition of revenge.

### Signal 3: Re-entry in the same instrument within minutes

A re-entry within 10–15 minutes of a stop-out in the same symbol, often in the opposite direction, is the clearest single sign. Real setups rarely re-form that fast.

### Signal 4: Outlier red days

**Your worst days should look like your normal losing days, just a bit bigger; if a few are 3–5 times your average losing day, those are almost always revenge days.**

*The following is a hypothetical account for illustration.*

| Measure | Value |
| :--- | ---: |
| Average losing day | −₹3,200 |
| Three worst days | −₹14,800, −₹12,100, −₹11,600 |
| Net P&L for the quarter | +₹9,400 |
| Net P&L excluding those three days | **+₹47,900** |

Three days erased 80% of a quarter's profit. On each of them, the daily journal entry starts with an early loss.

### Signal 5: Trades without a setup

If you tag trades by setup, count the untagged or `no-setup` trades. Revenge trades rarely belong to a playbook. If most of your no-setup trades come right after a loss, that's the pattern.

## Rules That Stop the Spiral

**Rules work against revenge trading only if they trigger mechanically, before the urge, not when you "feel" emotional.** Decide them when the market is closed.

| Rule | Why it works |
| :--- | :--- |
| 30-minute pause after any stop-out | Lets the urge pass; most revenge trades happen within minutes |
| No size increase on the next trade after a loss | Removes the "win it back faster" option |
| Daily loss limit of 2× your average losing day | Caps the outlier days that erase weeks |
| Stop after 3 consecutive losses | Three in a row usually means the market isn't offering your setup today |
| No same-symbol re-entry for 15 minutes | Kills the instant flip-and-chase |

Write these rules into your trading plan and keep them where you'll see them during the day. Some brokers let you lock yourself out: Zerodha's [kill switch](https://support.zerodha.com/category/console/segments/killswitch/articles/what-is-the-kill-switch) disables a segment such as F&O, and it can only be re-enabled after 12 hours. Dhan offers a similar option under Trader's Controls. Either one makes a daily limit enforceable rather than optional.

## Key Takeaways

- **Revenge trading is a cluster, not a trade:** one normal loss followed by several unplanned, larger, faster trades.
- **Find it in data:** post-loss trade frequency, post-loss size, quick same-symbol re-entries, outlier red days and no-setup trades.
- **A few outlier days can erase a quarter's profit.** Compare net P&L with and without them.
- **Rules must trigger mechanically:** a pause after a stop-out, no size increase, a daily loss limit, stop after 3 losses.
- **Re-check monthly.** Fewer outlier red days is the proof.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is revenge trading?

Revenge trading is taking unplanned, often larger trades to quickly recover a recent loss. It's driven by loss aversion rather than a trading setup, and it typically turns a normal losing trade into a large losing day.

### How do I stop revenge trading in F&O?

Set mechanical rules before the market opens: pause for 30 minutes after any stop-out, never increase size on the next trade after a loss, stop trading after three consecutive losses, and set a daily loss limit. Then check your tradebook monthly to confirm outlier red days are shrinking.
